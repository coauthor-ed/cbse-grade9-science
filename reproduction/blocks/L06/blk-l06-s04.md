# Why a Caterpillar?

<!-- STEP -->
## Food stored in the egg

Fish, amphibians and insects produce several hundred to several thousand eggs at a time. Each egg has **yolk**, a store of food for the developing embryo. The mother's body cannot provide large amounts of yolk for so many eggs.

<!-- STEP -->
## Just enough to hatch

The yolk in each egg is just enough to produce a **larva**, which hatches from the egg. A larva is a feeding stage. Some larvae eat organic wastes such as rotten food and manure, and a caterpillar eats leaves.

<!-- STEP -->
## A butterfly's life

Once a larva has stored enough food, a change takes place and the adult body forms. A butterfly goes through egg, larva, pupa, emergence and adult. Frogs go through a larva stage too.

<!-- STEP -->
## Enough yolk to hatch a young one

Reptiles and birds lay eggs too, but each egg contains enough yolk to nourish the embryo until it hatches into a young one. A chick has no larva stage.

<!-- STEP -->
## Inside the mother

In mammals, the zygote grows and develops inside the female body.

**An egg with little yolk gives a larva. An egg with more yolk gives a young one.**
