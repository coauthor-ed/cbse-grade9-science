# Eggs and Survival

<!-- STEP -->
## Most are lost

The new column shows the estimated survival of young ones: low for fish and frogs, moderate for lizards, and moderate to high for birds.

**Neither animal is careless or careful. A frog's eggs lie in open water, and a bird's develop in a protected way.**

<!-- STEP -->
## More eggs, less chance for each

Read down the two columns. Animals that produce the most eggs have the lowest chance for each young one. More eggs does not mean more young ones grow up.

<!-- STEP -->
## External fertilisation

Fish and frogs fertilise their eggs outside the body. Eggs lie where currents can carry them off and other animals can eat them, so many are lost.

<!-- STEP -->
## Internal fertilisation

In reptiles, birds and mammals, fertilisation takes place inside the body of the female. This is **internal fertilisation**. The fertilised egg or embryo is more protected, so each young one generally has a better chance.
