## Set up a test tube

Asexual reproduction is also seen in bacteria, amoeba, yeast, hydra and sponge. To see it in yeast, add a pinch of yeast granules to 20 mL of sugar solution (1 g in 10 mL) in a test tube. Put in a cotton plug and keep it warm.

<!-- STEP -->
## Look at one drop

After 1-2 hours, place a small drop on a glass slide, cover it with a coverslip and look through a compound microscope. You will see many small round cells.

<!-- STEP -->
## Small round outgrowths

Some cells carry small round outgrowths called **buds**. Is the yeast duplicating? Yes: a bud grows from the parent cell, enlarges and then separates. This is **budding**.

<!-- STEP -->
## A hydra buds too

A hydra is a multicellular animal. Repeated cell division at one site on its body makes a bud, which enlarges and separates to live on its own. One hydra often carries many buds at the same time.
