# Why does the day of ovulation change?

<!-- STEP -->
## Later, around day 21

Her egg is likely to be released around day 21. Many people expect day 14 every time, because that number is the one usually taught.

But in most cycles the egg is released about **14 days before the next period**. A longer cycle has a longer first half.

<!-- STEP -->
## Try three cycle lengths

Press **21 days**, **28 days** and **35 days** and watch the egg's day on the track.

- A 21-day cycle gives about day 7.
- A 28-day cycle gives about day 14.
- A 35-day cycle gives about day 21.

<!-- STEP -->
## "Always on day 14" is wrong

A student says that ovulation always happens on day 14. This claim is wrong for two reasons.

- Cycles run from 21 to 35 days, so the day moves with the cycle length.
- The timing can shift from one cycle to the next in the same person.

All these days are rough, so counting days is not a safe way to plan or prevent a pregnancy.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why periods stop in pregnancy

When a zygote implants, the lining is in use. Hormones then keep the lining in place, so it is not shed, and no more eggs are released.

**So the monthly cycle stops during pregnancy.**
