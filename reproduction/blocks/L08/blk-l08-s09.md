# How does life continue?

<!-- STEP -->
## New individuals of the same kind

Reproduction is the biological process by which living beings produce new individuals of their own kind. It carries genetic information forward, and it keeps life going. There are two main types: asexual and sexual.

<!-- STEP -->
## Asexual reproduction

Budding, spore formation and vegetative propagation all need one parent. Mitosis makes the new cells, so the offspring are almost exact copies.

<!-- STEP -->
## Sexual reproduction

Two parents make gametes by meiosis. In a flower, pollen travels from anther to stigma, and after fertilisation the ovule becomes the seed and the ovary becomes the fruit.

In humans, testes make sperm and ovaries make eggs, and a zygote grows in the uterus for about nine months.

<!-- STEP -->
## When does a farmer prefer which?

A farmer chooses asexual methods to get many copies of one good variety, quickly. All the plants then share the same weakness to a disease.

A farmer chooses seeds and sexual reproduction to bring in new mixes of characters, which helps when a new disease or a new climate arrives.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why do complex living things use sexual reproduction?

Think about what each way gives. Copying is fast when conditions are good, and that suits simple organisms like yeast and hydra.

Sexual reproduction brings variation, and variation helps a species to survive when surroundings change. That may be why most complex animals and flowering plants use it.

## A question still open

An amoeba or a yeast cell divides into two almost identical copies. Does a cell like this ever grow old? So, does ageing happen at all in such an organism?

Keep this question with you. Biologists still study it, and you may want to read about it.
