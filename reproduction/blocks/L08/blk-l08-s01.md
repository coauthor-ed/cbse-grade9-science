# What happens when an egg is not fertilised?

<!-- STEP -->
## Days 1 to 5: a period

If an egg is not fertilised, it stays viable for about a day and then degenerates. The lining starts to break down about two weeks after ovulation, and then it sheds. This shedding, on day 1 of the next cycle, is the period.

The lining, along with some blood, leaves the body through the vagina. This is **menstruation**, or a period, and it usually lasts 3 to 7 days.

<!-- STEP -->
## Days 6 to 14: the lining rebuilds

After a period, the lining of the uterus gradually rebuilds. At the same time an egg starts maturing in an ovary.

<!-- STEP -->
## Day 14: ovulation

In a typical 28-day cycle, the ovary releases a mature egg around day 14. You know this event as **ovulation**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Days 15 to 28: thicker again

The lining becomes thicker and rich in blood vessels, ready to receive a zygote.

If there is no fertilisation, the lining starts breaking down around day 28, and the cycle repeats.

<!-- STEP -->
## From puberty to menopause

This cycle repeats typically every 21 to 35 days, often around 28 days. It usually begins at puberty, between the ages of 10 and 14 in girls, and it continues till **menopause**, around age 50.

In the first years, periods can be irregular, and that is common.
