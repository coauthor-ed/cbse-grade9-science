# Five Bags, One Scissors, Three Pods

<!-- STEP -->
## What is on the bench

A row of pea plants, each carrying buds and open flowers. Next to them: five muslin cloth bags and one pair of scissors.

That is everything you get. Five bags. One scissors. No more arriving.

## Your target

Set three pods on this row, and be able to say why each treatment you spent was worth spending.

Every bag you fit and every cut you make is gone once you have made it. The plant cannot hand it back.

<!-- STEP -->
## What you can do to a flower

For any flower on the row you may do nothing, bag it, cut its stamens out, or cut its stamens out and then bag it.

A cut can only be made on a flower that is already open. A bud is closed, and there is nothing to reach in and take.

That is not a rule the game adds to make it fair. It is the whole reason the experiment in the last slide came out the way it did.

<!-- STEP -->
## What the game will not do for you

The panel will not tell you a treatment is right before you commit it. It tells you afterwards, and a flower that got nothing tells you nothing until you have already spent the bag.

You can spend them in any order. You can spend the same kind twice. There is no clock and nothing is deducted for a wrong call.

**Three pods, five bags, and no second chance at any single flower. Choose the way a gardener would.**
