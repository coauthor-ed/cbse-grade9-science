# Take a Flower Apart

<!-- STEP -->
## More than decoration

Leaves make food, roots take in water and stems hold a plant up. A flower can seem to have no such job, only colour and scent.

In fact **flowers are the reproductive organs of flowering plants**, also called angiosperms. Non-flowering plants like pines also reproduce sexually. Let us open a flower from the outside in, the way you can with a flower from your garden.

<!-- STEP -->
## The green covering

In a bud, a thin green covering wraps all the other parts. This outermost ring is made of **sepals**, and they protect the flower in the bud stage.

<!-- STEP -->
## The coloured ring

Inside the sepals are the coloured **petals**. Many are also scented, and we will soon see who notices that colour and scent.

<!-- STEP -->
## The male part

Next comes the **stamen**, the male part of the flower. A thin stalk, the **filament**, holds up a head, the **anther**. The anther produces pollen grains, and pollen grains contain the male gametes.

<!-- STEP -->
## The female part

The centre holds the **pistil**, the female part. At its tip is the **stigma**, which may be flat or sticky. Below it runs the **style**, a thin long tube.

<!-- STEP -->
## Inside the swollen base

The style ends in the **ovary**, the swollen base of the pistil. The ovary contains **ovules**, and each ovule has an **egg cell**, the female gamete.

**The male gametes are in the pollen and the egg cell is deep inside the ovary. How do they ever meet?**
