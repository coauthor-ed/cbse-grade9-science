# Do Bees Help Apple Trees?

<!-- STEP -->
## A problem in the hills

Apples are an important cash crop in the lower Himalayan region. Yields are falling, and this is linked with climate change and fewer natural pollinators. So a group of researchers and farmers set up orchards at two places.

At **Place A**, natural pollinators did the work. At **Place B**, the farmers also kept bees.

<!-- STEP -->
## What were they testing?

Their **hypothesis**, a statement that a test can check, was that keeping bees in an orchard gives a better apple yield. They measured two things:

- **Fruit set:** fruits as a percentage of fruit-bearing branches
- **Fruit drop:** the percentage of young fruits that fall early

<!-- STEP -->
## The results

Here are the numbers, read from the study's graph. Fruit set was 26 per cent at Place A and 40 per cent at Place B. Fruit drop was 35 per cent at Place A and only 8 per cent at Place B. So Place B did better on both measures.

<!-- STEP -->
## Count the apples

Suppose each orchard has 200 fruit-bearing branches. This is a made-up number, chosen to make the sums easy. Place A sets 52 fruits and drops about 18, leaving about 34. Place B sets 80 and drops about 6, leaving about 74.

**We can infer that keeping bees helped, and those farmers got honey as well. The two places could differ in other ways, so one trial does not prove it.**
