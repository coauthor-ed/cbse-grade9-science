# What Carries the Pollen?

<!-- STEP -->
## Pollen for each seed

The insect-pollinated plant uses far less. Divide the pollen by the seeds: the grass uses about 2,500 to 20,000 grains for each seed, and the insect-pollinated plant about 20 to 50.

**More pollen does not mean more seeds.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Wind

Pollination often depends on outside agents called **pollinators**. Wind carries the pollen of wheat, maize and rice, and these plants are called wind-pollinated. The grains are light and small, and the stigma is long and feathery, so it traps grains from the air.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why so much pollen can still work

Wind does not carry grains to any particular flower, so most land elsewhere and are lost. A stigma catches only the grains that pass it. With lakhs of grains in the air, enough of them reach stigmas for seeds to form.

<!-- STEP -->
## Water

In water plants such as *Vallisneria* and *Hydrilla*, currents carry the pollen from one flower to another.

<!-- STEP -->
## Insects

Sunflower, hibiscus and marigold are pollinated by insects such as bees and butterflies. These flowers are often bright, with nectar and fragrance. The grains are large, sticky or spiny, so they attach to the insect's body, and the sticky stigma receives them.

<!-- STEP -->
## Birds

Some flowers, like those of the coral tree and hibiscus, are pollinated by birds such as the Indian white-eye and sunbirds. Hibiscus is on both lists because insects and birds both visit it.
