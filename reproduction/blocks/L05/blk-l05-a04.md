# Getting the Grains Where They Count

The field in front of you has to be pollinated, and the rules change halfway through.

<!-- STEP -->
## First, the maize patch

A block of maize. The tassels at the top shake loose a continuous spray of pollen, and it drifts sideways on the wind.

The stigmas below are long and feathery, so they catch a share of everything that passes them. **You do not have to aim.** Spray, and let the field do the rest.

Every flower you see is a target, and only a fraction of what you release will ever stick.

<!-- STEP -->
## Then, the insect patch

Clear the maize and the same field comes back - this time as insect-pollinated plants.

Now the stigmas are small and sticky, and the pollen is large and spiny. **You have to land each grain on a specific flower**, one at a time, and the rest of the spray is simply lost on the ground.

Same field. Same target. A completely different way of hitting it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the rules change

That swap is the whole lesson in one gesture.

A wide feathery stigma and a huge shower of tiny grains is one design: **scatter, and sweep up a fraction.** A small sticky stigma and large clinging grains is another: **deliver, and miss nothing.**

Both reach the same end point - pollen on a receptive stigma, and a seed after that. Which one a plant uses depends on what the plant is built to offer, and whether anything is willing to come and collect it.

**So: which is your field doing, and does it match what the flowers in it are offering?**
