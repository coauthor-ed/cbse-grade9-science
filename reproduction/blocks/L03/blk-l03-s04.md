## The pairs line up

The cell copies each chromosome, as it does before mitosis. Then the chromosomes come together in their pairs, side by side. Three pairs stand for all 23 here.

<!-- STEP -->
## The pairs separate

In the first division, the two chromosomes of each pair move apart. Each new cell gets one chromosome from each pair.

<!-- STEP -->
## The copies split

In a second division, each chromosome splits from its copy. Two divisions give four cells, each with one chromosome from every pair.

<!-- STEP -->
## Names for the two numbers

A cell with the full set is **diploid**, and a cell with half is **haploid**. Haploid cells used only for reproduction are **gametes**: sperm and eggs in animals. In plants, the pollen grain contains male gametes, and the ovule contains the egg.
