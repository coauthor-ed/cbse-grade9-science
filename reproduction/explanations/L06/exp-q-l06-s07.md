# Eggs Is Not Young Ones

The mistake this question is built on is treating a large number of eggs as a large number of survivors. Those are two different quantities, and only one of them goes up when the other does.

**What happens to an egg in open water.** It is attached to nothing. A current carries it, and anything that eats in that water can eat it. A fish or a frog cannot be in every place its eggs are at once, so most of the eggs laid there do not reach hatching. The large number is the price of leaving them there.

**What happens to an egg held inside a body.** It is not drifting anywhere and nothing can reach it. The animal can afford to make only a few, and nearly all of them survive.

**So the two animals are not being judged on the same thing.** One is measured by the attempt it made and the other by the young ones it actually produced. Reading the table that way, the row with the most eggs has the lowest survival and the row with the fewest has the highest.

**Egg number and the number surviving move in opposite directions. That is the whole of the lesson's table.**
