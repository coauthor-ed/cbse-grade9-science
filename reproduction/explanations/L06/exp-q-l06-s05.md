# Two Places for the Same Job

An animal's young one cannot be built out of nothing. It needs food to grow with, and that food has to come from somewhere between the egg and the adult body.

**The fish does the feeding after hatching.** Its eggs carry only a little yolk, so what hatches is a larva - a feeding stage, not a young fish. The larva eats organic waste, which is available almost everywhere and requires no hunting, and stores what it takes in. Once enough has accumulated, the adult body forms. That is the butterfly's egg, larva, pupa, adult run: the larva and pupa exist because the egg could not carry enough on its own.

**The lizard does the feeding before hatching.** Each of its few eggs is stocked with enough yolk to carry the embryo all the way to a young lizard. Nothing has to find food after it hatches, so there is no larva and no pupa.

The total job is the same in both cases - supplying food from the egg to the adult body. Only the place it is done differs: outside, in open water and then in the larva, or inside, packed into a few well-stocked eggs.

**And that is why the numbers differ.** A fish cannot put much food into hundreds of eggs, so it must make many eggs and let most of them go. A lizard can put a lot of food into each egg, so it needs only a few, and can afford to hold and guard them.
