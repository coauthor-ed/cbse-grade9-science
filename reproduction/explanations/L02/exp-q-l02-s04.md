# Why Many Buds at Once

## The one site

A bud on a hydra is not a separate organism that arrived from outside. It is an outgrowth of the parent's own body, produced where **repeated cell division keeps happening at one fixed site**.

That is the whole answer to the question, and it sits in one clause.

## Why the site does not switch off

Nothing about making one bud uses up the site. The division at that spot can begin again while the first bud is still attached and still growing.

So the hydra is not producing buds one after another. It is producing them **simultaneously**, at the same place on its body, each from its own repeated division.

## The three wrong answers, in turn

- **A separate tube feeds each bud.** There is no such tube. A bud is continuous with the parent's body tissue while it grows - which is exactly why it is an outgrowth and not a visitor.
- **Buds are spores from the water.** A spore is a free body that travels and settles; it stays free for its whole life. A bud stays joined until it deliberately separates, and it separates *away* from the parent to live on its own.
- **The parent splits into as many parts as there are buds.** The parent does not shrink or divide itself apart. Each bud keeps only what it has already made and then leaves with its own body column and its own tentacles.

## The pattern to carry forward

A bud forms, enlarges, separates, lives independently - and the site that made it keeps working. That is the rule, and it holds for the yeast cell on the slide as well as for the whole animal.
