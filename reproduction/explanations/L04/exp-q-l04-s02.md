# Why the Bud Failed and the Open Flower Did Not

## Start with what the bag can do

Both flowers were bagged, so pollen arriving from outside is ruled out for both. Whatever separates them, the bag is not doing it.

That leaves only one difference between the two treatments: the bud had not opened yet, and the open flower had.

## The bud had not been pollinated at all

A bud is a closed package. Its petals have not unfolded, so nothing has arrived on its stigma from anywhere - and nothing had arrived from inside either, because the flower had not reached the stage where it does that.

Then two things happen in the same afternoon. The stamens are cut out, so the flower can no longer make any pollen. And the bag goes on, so no pollen can arrive from outside.

Both routes are closed. This flower has no way of being pollinated, and so it has no way of making a pod.

## The open flower had already finished with its stamens

The open flower had been open for a day or more before anyone touched it. During that time, while it was still a bud, it had been pollinated from its own stamens.

Then the petals unfolded, and the bag went on, and the stamens were cut away. All three of those happened after the pollination. Cutting the stamens off a flower that is already pollinated removes a part the flower has finished using - it prevents nothing.

## So the failing treatment is the more thorough one

The bud is the only one of the two you could actually open up and clean out. It is the treatment that looks most complete, and it is the one that gives nothing.

**A bud pollinates itself before it opens. The more thoroughly you strip a bud, the more certain you are that nothing will ever reach it.**
