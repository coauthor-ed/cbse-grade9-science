## What If Rock Layers Alone Could Prove Ancestry?

This lesson uses undisturbed rock layers to show that a fossil in a lower layer is older than one above it - a real, supported claim.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- Layer order alone would only show that one fossil is older than another - it would not show that the older organism is a direct ancestor of the younger one, since order and ancestry are two separate claims.
- Proving direct ancestry needs more than order: genuine anatomical or genetic evidence actually connecting the two organisms, which layering by itself never supplies.
- If age alone proved ancestry, any two organisms found in the right layer order - even completely unrelated ones - could get wrongly called ancestor and descendant just for happening to be in sequence.

Rock layers can support "this came before that." Turning that into "this is the direct ancestor of that" needs evidence the layers alone were never going to supply.
