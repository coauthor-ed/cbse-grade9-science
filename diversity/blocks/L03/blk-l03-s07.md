## White-Green Patches

You may have seen white-green patches on a tree trunk, a damp wall or a stone. These are lichens. A lichen is two organisms living together: an autotrophic alga and a heterotrophic fungus.

<!-- STEP -->
## What Each Partner Gives

The alga photosynthesises and provides food. The fungus provides protection. Both gain from this, so it is a mutual association, which your book calls symbiotic.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Natural Air Check

Elderly people in many villages see these patches as a sign of clean air. Lichens change their colour with air pollutants, so researchers can use the colour to find out how much pollutant is in the air.

This makes lichens natural bioindicators: living things that tell us about air quality.

<!-- STEP -->
## Useful, With Care

Villagers collect lichens as forest produce. Some, commonly called patthar ke phool, are used as a spice, and some as medicines. Others have been used since ancient times to dye wool and silk maroon, violet or burgundy.

**Some lichens are poisonous, so they must be classified and identified properly before use.**
