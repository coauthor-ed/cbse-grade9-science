## What If Every Bacterium Behaved Like a Disease-Causing One?

This lesson kept separating a bacterium's kingdom (Monera - no nucleus) from its actual job, which can be very different from one bacterium to the next.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- Dahi-making would stop. Lactobacillus is the bacterium that turns milk into curd every day in Indian kitchens - treating it as dangerous just for being a bacterium would end a harmless, useful process.
- Farmers would lose real help. Rhizobium partners with plant roots to pull nitrogen from the air, keeping soil healthy - assuming every soil bacterium is harmful would mean giving that up for no real reason.
- Ram Bux Singh's 1957 biogas plant would make no sense to build. It runs entirely on bacteria breaking down waste into fuel - useful work that "bacterium equals germ" thinking would wrongly rule out from the start.

Some bacteria really do cause disease - that's true. But treating that as true of the whole kingdom would erase curd, healthy soil, and a working biogas plant, all at once.
