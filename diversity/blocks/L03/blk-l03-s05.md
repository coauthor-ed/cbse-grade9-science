## One Cell, Three Ways to Move

Protista holds single-celled eukaryotes that live in water or moist places. You can see three common ones here, and each moves in its own way.

- Amoeba pushes out parts of its body and flows into them.
- Paramecium beats many tiny hairs called cilia.
- Euglena beats a whip-like tail called a flagellum.

<!-- STEP -->
## Making Food Does Not Mean Plant

Some protists are autotrophic and others are heterotrophic. Euglena can be both. It photosynthesises in the light and absorbs food in the dark. Try both with the **Light** and **Dark** buttons.

Euglena makes food, but it is one cell, so it is a protist and not a plant.

## A Link in the Water

Protists are an important link in food chains in water. Some produce oxygen, some are food for small animals, and some are decomposers that help recycle nutrients.

## Pause and Ponder

How can one cell carry out every life process, when your body uses billions of cells for the same jobs?

Think about these two points:

- Amoeba's one cell moves, feeds and grows. In your body, different cells do these jobs.
- Every part of that cell is close to the water around it.
