## Threads You Do Not See

Fungi are mostly multicellular eukaryotes with cell walls made of chitin, and they do not make their own food. Under a mushroom, fine threads spread through dead and decaying matter. Together these threads form a network called mycelium, and nutrients are absorbed through them.

<!-- STEP -->
## Fungi Clean Up the Forest

Most fungi are saprophytes: they feed on dead organic matter, such as fallen leaves, twigs or dead organisms. They break it down into simpler substances, which leaves minerals in the soil.

**Without fungi, dead matter would decay far less and the soil would be less fertile.**

## Spores, Partners and Parasites

Fungi often reproduce by forming spores, and they grow best in warm and moist conditions. Not all of them feed on dead matter. Some live in a mutually helpful relationship with other organisms, and others are parasites that cause diseases in plants and animals.

<!-- STEP -->
## Useful Fungi

Aspergillus and Penicillium are used to make enzymes and antibiotics. Mushroom farming is also a growing way to earn a living, because it needs little space and low investment, and its life cycle takes only 30 to 45 days.

## Wild Mushrooms

Wild edible mushrooms are valuable food, and some are used as medicine. Many communities in India, including tribal communities, use and conserve them, and they hold traditional knowledge of which ones are edible and which are poisonous.

Shape does not tell you which is which. Never eat a wild mushroom by how it looks.
