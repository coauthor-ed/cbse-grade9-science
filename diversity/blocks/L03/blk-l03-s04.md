## Everywhere, Even Inside You

Monera holds the bacteria and the cyanobacteria, all single-celled prokaryotes. Bacteria live in soil, water and air, and in hot springs where most organisms cannot survive. They are present inside the human body too.

They come in a few shapes: round, rod, comma and spiral.

## Germs Are Only One Part

Many people hear "bacteria" and think only of germs. Some bacteria are harmful: these pathogens cause diseases. But many are useful.

- Lactobacillus turns milk into curd.
- Rhizobium lives in the roots of pea and bean plants and helps them get nitrogen.
- Some bacteria recycle nutrients, and some break down pollutants such as oil, pesticides and sewage.

## Bacteria That Make Food

Cyanobacteria carry out photosynthesis, so they are autotrophs. A plant is not the only thing that makes food: this is one cell with no nucleus. The decomposer work in Monera is done by other bacteria.

## Biogas From Dung

Bacteria also live in the gut of ruminants, the animals that chew the cud, such as cows. They are responsible for making biogas from the dung of these animals.

In 1957, Ram Bux Singh set up India's first scientifically designed biogas plant at Ramnagar, Sitapur, Uttar Pradesh. He later worked on low-cost plants for rural areas, and he is known as the 'Father of Modern Biogas'.
