## A Physical Barrier: Mangroves

Forests rich in life can protect us in three ways, and the first is physical. When the super cyclone hit Odisha in 1999 (your book writes Orissa), villages with more mangroves experienced less destruction.

## A Biological Barrier: Many Hosts

Your book says that the risk of tick-borne disease is generally lower where biodiversity is rich. In the Western Ghats, forest diversity acts as a barrier against Monkey Fever (Kyasanur Forest Disease), because the virus cannot multiply in many of the animals there.

## A Chemical Barrier: Soil Microbes

Diverse microorganisms in forest soils and plant roots absorb, transform or break down pollutants, and this improves water quality. Mangrove soils also trap sediments and heavy metals, which stops them spreading into oceans and rivers.

**A rich forest lowers a risk. It does not remove it.**
