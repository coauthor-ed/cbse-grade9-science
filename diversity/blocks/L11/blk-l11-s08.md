## Life at a Glance

Life on Earth exists in many forms, from simple bacteria to complex animals. Scientists classify organisms by cell structure, number of cells, mode of nutrition and ecological role, and Whittaker’s five kingdoms are a simple and useful way to understand this diversity.

<!-- STEP -->
## The Big Branches

- **Plantae:** Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta, Gymnosperm and Angiosperm.
- **Non-chordata:** Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca and Echinodermata.
- **Chordata:** protochordates, with a notochord at least once in life, and the vertebrates: fish, amphibians, reptiles, birds and mammals.

## Try One Yourself

Choose one of these to do outside class:

- Visit a park or water body, identify ten organisms and classify them into kingdoms.
- Ask a gardener or forest worker how they identify species.
- Collect stamps of plants and animals and classify them by your own criteria.
- Study an extinct species, and what happened to the organisms that depended on it.
- Plan how you would organise information about the bird species of a sanctuary such as Pakke.
- Study the diversity of farm animals used in different types of farming in India.

Keep to a safe place, stay away from nests, collect nothing without a teacher, and never taste a wild mushroom or lichen.

## A Tree Still Growing

Classification is not just about naming organisms. It helps us understand how they are related, trace the history of life on Earth and conserve biodiversity.

How do you protect something you cannot name? You now hold the names, and scientists are still adding to this tree. What might they find next, and how would it change the tree?
