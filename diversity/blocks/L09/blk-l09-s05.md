## One Genus, Two Species

The tiger and the lion are in one genus, *Panthera*, the roaring cats. They can roar and have a similar skull structure. A genus groups closely related species that share common features, but these two are still separate species.

<!-- STEP -->
## What a Species Is

A species is a group of similar individuals that can interbreed and produce offspring, and those offspring can have young of their own. Sharing a genus does not make two animals one species.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Name in Two Parts

Carolus Linnaeus introduced **binomial nomenclature** in the 18th century. Every organism has a scientific name of two parts, in Latin or a Latinised form: the genus first, then the species name. Together they form one name, used all over the world.

<!-- STEP -->
## How to Write It

The genus begins with a capital letter, and the species name is in small letters. In print the name is in italics. By hand it is underlined, each word on its own. Press **Handwriting** to see this.
