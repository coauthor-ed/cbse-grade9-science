## Green Mats on Damp Walls

Mosses and liverworts such as Marchantia belong to Bryophyta. You may have seen them as green mats on damp rocks, old walls or soil, especially during the monsoons.

Their body is a little more divided than a thallus, with simple stem-like and leaf-like parts.

<!-- STEP -->
## Rhizoids Are Not Roots

Below the moss are root-like threads called rhizoids. They hold it to the surface, but they are not true roots. Bryophytes also have no vascular tissue to carry water and food through the body, so they survive only in moist, shady places.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why They Stay Near Water

Bryophytes need water to reproduce, because male reproductive cells must swim to reach female cells. Press **Dry the surface** to see what that means.

**This is why bryophytes are called the 'amphibians' of the plant kingdom: they live on land, but they depend on moisture.**
