## What If Spirogyra Were the Size of a Tree?

Spirogyra is a simple thread, one cell wide, with every cell already touching the water around it directly - no transport network needed.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- Cells at the centre of a tree-sized thread would no longer be close enough to the outside water to exchange directly - the exact advantage that makes Spirogyra's simple body work would disappear as soon as it grew past a certain size.
- A supply problem would show up first in the middle of that giant thread, farthest from any wet surface - the same kind of problem xylem and phloem exist to solve in a fern.
- A body that size would need something like transport tissue just to survive at all, well before it ever attempted reproduction - showing that transport tissue solves a size problem, not a reproduction problem.

Spirogyra's simple, transport-free body works precisely because it stays small and stays in water. Size is exactly what a fern's xylem and phloem had to solve for.
