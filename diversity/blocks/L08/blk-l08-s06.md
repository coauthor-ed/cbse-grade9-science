## Bodies That Changed

The animals we see today are the outcome of changes in body structure over very long periods of time. Fish are one example: fins and gills allow them to move and breathe in water.

<!-- STEP -->
## Feathers and Hollow Bones

Feathers and hollow bones make flight possible in birds. Wing feathers give a broad surface, and hollow bones keep the skeleton light. The ostrich has both feathers and wings and still cannot fly, so these features do not promise flight.

<!-- STEP -->
## Fat in the Hump

A camel's hump stores fat, not water. Where food is hard to find, this fat is a store of energy for the camel's body. A camel still needs to drink.

<!-- STEP -->
## Air in the Fur

A polar bear's thick fur traps a layer of air near its skin. The air slows down the loss of heat from the body, which helps the bear to survive on the ice.

<!-- STEP -->
## Milk Helps the Young Survive

Mammary glands are one more structural change, and they improve the survival of young ones. A young mammal drinks milk before it can find food of its own, on land or in the sea.

<!-- STEP -->
## Nobody Chose This

A polar bear did not decide to grow thick fur. Young animals inherit features from their parents. Over many generations, an inherited feature that helps animals survive and have young becomes more common. **An animal does not choose its adaptations.**
