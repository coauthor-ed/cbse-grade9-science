## What If Only Habitat Were Recorded?

Imagine a record that only ever wrote down where each animal lives - never its body covering, its breathing structure, or whether it feeds its young milk.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- A dolphin (water) and a tiger (land) would be sorted into completely different groups by a habitat-only record - even though both actually share mammary glands, milk-feeding and lungs, the real evidence that places them together in Mammalia.
- The record would be missing exactly the decisive evidence this lesson relies on to tell the five vertebrate classes apart: body covering, breathing structure, and milk-feeding.
- Two animals from totally different vertebrate classes that happen to share a habitat - say, a fish and a diving bird - could look deceptively "the same" on a habitat-only record, with nothing to actually tell them apart.

Habitat can be a genuinely useful clue to start with - this lesson just keeps showing why it can never be the whole record.
