## What If a Starfish's Plates Counted as a Notochord?

A starfish has real internal support - hard calcium-carbonate plates - but this lesson is careful to say that isn't the same thing as a notochord.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- A starfish would suddenly count as a chordate, purely for having some kind of hard internal support - even though its plates are a completely different kind of structure from a flexible rod.
- The real test was never "is there something hard inside?" - it's specifically whether a flexible, rod-shaped structure runs the length of the body, and a starfish's plates simply are not that.
- Echinodermata and Chordata would lose their reason to be two separate groups, even though their actual support structures are genuinely different: rigid plates in one, a flexible rod in the other.

Keeping "has internal support," "has a notochord," and "has a backbone" as three separate questions is exactly what stops a starfish's plates from being mistaken for something they're not.
