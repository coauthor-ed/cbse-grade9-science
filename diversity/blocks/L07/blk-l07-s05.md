## A Hard Outer Skeleton

An arthropod has an **exoskeleton**, a hard skeleton on the outside. It protects the body, reduces water loss and supports powerful muscles, so arthropods can survive in dry and exposed places.

Press **Bend the leg** to see the muscle pull on the hard parts.

## Jointed Legs, Specialised Segments

Different segments of an arthropod do different jobs. The name points to the legs: in Greek, *arthron* means joint and *pous* means foot, which fits your book's "jointed appendages".

<!-- STEP -->
## A Shell Is Not a Joint

A snail also has a hard outer covering, but it moves on one muscular foot and has no jointed legs. It belongs to **Mollusca**: soft bodies with a distinct head, a muscular foot and a hump, and in many of them a protective shell.

Your book also prints "segmented" here, but most molluscs show no segments.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Mollusc With No Outer Shell

An octopus has no outer shell at all, and its foot has become arms and a funnel. Snails, squids and octopuses share one basic body plan, changed in different directions in different environments.

**A shell alone does not decide the phylum.**
