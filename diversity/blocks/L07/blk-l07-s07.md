## Eight Phyla Side by Side

Here are all eight phyla in your book's order. Three questions compare them: where does it live, how is its body organised, and what skeleton does it have?

Tap **Habitat**, **Level** or **Skeleton** to read one row across all eight.

## From Cells to Organ Systems

Sponges have a cellular level of organisation, Cnidaria a tissue level, and flatworms an organ level. The other five phyla work at the organ system level.

<!-- STEP -->
## Three Kinds of Skeleton

Your book lists no skeleton for the first five phyla. Arthropods and molluscs have an exoskeleton, and echinoderms have an endoskeleton, which is a skeleton inside the body.

<!-- STEP -->
## New Features, New Challenges

From sponges to echinoderms, body structure shows a pattern of increasing complexity. New features improve feeding, movement and protection, but they often bring new challenges.

This helps explain survival in different environments. Sponges are alive today in the sea, so the pattern is not a ladder of better and worse.

<!-- STEP -->
## A Rod Without a Backbone

Amphioxus is a protochordate. It has a notochord at least once during its life and no backbone, so it stands outside these eight phyla.

The notochord gives internal support without restricting movement.
