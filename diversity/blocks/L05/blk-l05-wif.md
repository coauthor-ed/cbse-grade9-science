## What If Seed Dispersal Happened Before Pollination?

This lesson keeps pollination and seed dispersal as two separate journeys - pollen moving to a flower, and a finished seed moving away from the parent plant.

Working through what that would actually mean:

- There would be nothing to disperse yet. A seed doesn't exist until pollination and fertilisation have already succeeded, so "seed dispersal first" has no seed to work with.
- Wind or an animal would have nothing to carry, since fertilisation - the event that actually produces a seed - would not have happened yet.
- Both journeys sometimes use the same carriers, like wind or a bee, which is exactly why it's easy to blur "moving pollen to a flower" with "moving a finished seed away" - even though one has to happen strictly before the other can even begin.

Swapping the order breaks the whole idea: a seed is the *result* of pollination succeeding, not something that can travel before pollination has even happened.
