## Land, Water and Air

Scientists built each system from the knowledge they had. Around the 4th century BCE, Aristotle grouped animals by habitat: land, water and air. This relied mainly on what could be seen from outside, so it told little about how organisms are built.

<!-- STEP -->
## Each System Solved One Problem

- **1758, Linnaeus:** two kingdoms, Plantae and Animalia.
- **1866, Haeckel:** Protista added, for unicellular organisms.
- **1938, Copeland:** Monera added, for organisms with no true nucleus.
- **1969, Whittaker:** Fungi added, giving five kingdoms.

## Grouping for Other Questions

The Rigveda and the Brihat Samhita classify animals by habitat (terrestrial, aquatic and aerial), by behaviour and by ecological role. These groupings answer other questions, such as where an animal lives and what it does.

**What you group by depends on what you want to know.**
