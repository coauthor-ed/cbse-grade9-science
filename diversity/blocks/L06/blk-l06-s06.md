## A Body for Many Places

Roundworms belong to the phylum **Nematoda**. A long, cylindrical body moves well through soil, through water or through a host's tissues, so roundworms can live in a wide range of places.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two Openings

A roundworm has a mouth at one end and an anus at the other, so food travels in one direction and waste leaves by a different opening.

These parts form an **organ system**, which is a group of organs working together on a larger job. Here the job is digestion.

## Not Every Worm Is a Parasite

It is easy to think that all worms are alike, and that all of them live inside other animals. Flatworms and roundworms are two separate phyla.

Each phylum has parasites, and each has many worms that live freely in water or soil. **Counting openings does not tell you whether a worm is a parasite.**

## Keeping Worms Out

Some infectious diseases are caused by parasitic worms. They enter our bodies through contaminated water and food, live especially in the digestive system, and take nutrients from us.

For this reason, your book suggests that you:

- wash your hands properly
- maintain personal hygiene and good sanitary habits
- eat properly cooked food
- drink boiled or filtered water
