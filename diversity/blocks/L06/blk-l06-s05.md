## Two Matching Halves

Flatworms belong to the phylum **Platyhelminthes**. They show **bilateral symmetry**: one plane divides the body into two matching halves.

The body has a head end and a tail end, so the worm moves head first. This better coordination is the beginning of directional movement.

<!-- STEP -->
## Flat Enough for Gases

A flatworm has no special organs for breathing. Its body is so flat that gases can pass in and out by diffusion.

<!-- STEP -->
## Organs, but Still One Opening

An **organ** is made of different tissues that do one job together. Flatworms have reached this organ level, and in many of them a muscular tube called the pharynx takes in food.

Yet there is still a single opening for food and waste, as in Hydra.

## Hooks and Suckers

Many flatworms are parasites: they live in or on another organism, called the host. Hooks and suckers hold them firmly to the host's tissues while they take nutrients from it.

Tapeworms go further and have no gut at all. They absorb food through the body surface.
