## The Water Moves Instead

Plants make it easy to think that staying still means making food, but a sponge does not make food. Sponges belong to the phylum **Porifera**, the pore-bearers, because numerous pores cover the body.

Water flows in through the pores all the time. It leaves through a larger opening.

<!-- STEP -->
## Food Comes to Each Cell

The moving water brings food particles and oxygen directly to individual cells, and it carries their waste away.

**A sponge stays still and still feeds on others, because the water does the moving.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Many Cells, No Tissues

A sponge is multicellular, but its cells are not organised into tissues or organs. It works by moving a great deal of water: one kilogram of sponge can filter up to 24,000 litres of sea water a day.

<!-- STEP -->
## Could It Live on Land?

Your book asks if a sponge could survive on land. Press **Stop the water** and watch what stops reaching the cells.

Without moving water, nothing brings food or oxygen and nothing takes the waste away. So a sponge could not survive on land.
