## Heads Out, Tails In

Press **Let them settle** in the picture. Water repels the tails, so with water on both sides a single layer cannot work: one row of tails would touch water. In two layers, the heads stay next to the water and the tails stay away from it.

This double sheet is called the **lipid bilayer**: two layers of special fat molecules, with heads outwards and tails inwards.

<!-- STEP -->
## Not a Solid Skin

You may picture a membrane as a fixed sheet, like thin plastic. The lipids are not fixed. Press **Sideways**, **Flip** and **Rotate** to follow one lipid.

The molecules can move sideways, flip and rotate within the membrane, and that is why we call the membrane **fluid**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Proteins in the Bilayer

Proteins are embedded in the lipid bilayer. Your book compares them to gatekeepers, because they help substances pass through. Press **Pass through** to see this.

A real gatekeeper thinks and chooses, but a protein does not. The word is only a comparison.

<!-- STEP -->
## Tiles in a Mosaic

Press **View from above**. Seen from outside the cell, heads and proteins lie side by side, like tiles in a mosaic floor.

**So the fluid-mosaic model has two parts: fluid because the molecules move, mosaic because they are arranged like tiles.**
