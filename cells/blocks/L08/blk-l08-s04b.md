# A beam of electrons

<!-- STEP -->
## Beyond light

Electron microscopes use a beam of electrons instead of light. They give highly magnified images, and they show the structure of a cell at the nanometre scale. A nanometre is one-billionth of a metre.

<!-- STEP -->
## A leaf at this scale

One picture made with a scanning electron microscope shows the lower surface of a Colocasia (arbi) leaf. The small pores called stomata are easy to see, and the scale bar on the picture marks just 0.05 mm.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two microscopes, two jobs

| | Light microscope | Electron microscope |
|---|---|---|
| Uses | Visible light | A beam of electrons |
| Smallest detail | About 200 nm | Close to 0.1 nm |
| Specimen | Can be alive | Not alive |
| Found in | School laboratories | Research laboratories |

**The stronger tool is not always the right one: it depends on what you want to see.**
