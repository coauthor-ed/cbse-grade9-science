# How small is a cell?

<!-- STEP -->
## One scale, from 1 km to 0.1 nm

Each main step down this scale is ten times smaller than the step above it. Near the bottom we need small units: 1 mm is 1000 micrometres (µm), and 1 µm is 1000 nanometres (nm).

<!-- STEP -->
## What the unaided eye reaches

Your eye reaches down to about 0.1 mm. Some cells are large enough to fall inside this range: a fish egg is one cell, and an amoeba is close to the limit. So not every cell is too small to see.

<!-- STEP -->
## What a light microscope adds

Most plant and animal cells are about 10 to 100 µm across, at or below the reach of the eye. A light microscope shows them, along with the nucleus, larger organelles such as mitochondria, and most bacteria. It stops at about 200 nm.

<!-- STEP -->
## What needs an electron microscope

Viruses, ribosomes, proteins and lipids are smaller still. An electron microscope reaches them, and it goes down to the size of atoms.
