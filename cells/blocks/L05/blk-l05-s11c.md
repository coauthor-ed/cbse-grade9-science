## Two Hard Names, Two Greek Roots

These two names look difficult, but each one is a short description. *Karyon* means nucleus. Your book gives *pro* as primitive and *eu* as true.

- **Prokaryotic** cells have only a primitive nucleus: no well-defined nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles. Bacteria are an example.
- **Eukaryotic** cells have a true nucleus, a well-defined one, and several membrane-bound organelles. Plant and animal cells are examples.

## The Two Kinds Compared

In prokaryotic cells, most activities take place directly in the cytoplasm.

| | Prokaryotic | Eukaryotic |
|---|---|---|
| Primitive nucleus (nucleoid) | Present | Absent |
| Typical diameter | 1 to 10 µm | 10 to 100 µm |
| Number of cells | Usually one | One or many |
| Membrane-bound organelles | Absent | Present |
| Membrane-bound nucleus | Absent | Present |

The primitive nucleus of a prokaryotic cell is called the **nucleoid**: a region that holds the genetic material, with no membrane around it.
