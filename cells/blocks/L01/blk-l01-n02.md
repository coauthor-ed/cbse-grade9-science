# What lives in a hot spring?

<!-- STEP -->
## Steam in a cold valley

This drawing shows a hot spring in the Puga Valley. The air is cold and the water is nearly boiling. If you said that nothing could live here, that was a fair guess, because boiling water kills most living things we know.

<!-- STEP -->
## Life in the hot water

A lens shows that the water is not empty. The organisms here are mostly **thermophiles**, which means heat-loving bacteria. Each one is a single cell, so we call it **unicellular**.

**This hot water holds living cells.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why scientists come here

Conditions in these springs seem to be similar to those on the early Earth, about 3.5 billion years ago. So a spring like this may hold clues about how the first cells began, and that raises a bigger question: where did life begin?
