# A Bottle That Must Be Right

## Salt in a drip bottle

A saline drip in a hospital is usually 0.9 % sodium chloride, which is common salt, in water. That means 0.9 g of salt in every 100 mL of solution. This proportion is safe for blood and replaces fluid the body has lost.

Use the two sliders to change the salt and the amount of solution, and try to match the label.

If your amounts differ, the liquid is still a clear salt solution. It is just not the solution the label promises.

<!-- STEP -->
## Solute and solvent

The salt is the **solute**, the substance that gets dissolved. The water is the **solvent**, the substance that dissolves it. Together they make the **solution**.

**Solute + solvent = solution.**

<!-- STEP -->
## How much is dissolved?

The amount of solute dissolved in a given amount of solvent or solution is called the **concentration** of the solution.

Saline is not the only case where concentration matters. A farmer mixes pesticide with water before spraying. Too little may not protect the crop, while too much can damage the crop, the soil and the environment.
