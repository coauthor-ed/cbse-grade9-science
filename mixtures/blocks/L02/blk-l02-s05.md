# Reading Three Labels

<!-- STEP -->
## Mass by mass on a pouch

Your textbook shows three packets, and each one uses a different kind of percentage.

Milk powder is not a solution. It is a heterogeneous mixture, but % m/m works for it too, and the table on the pouch gives each part "per 100 g". So 26 g of fat per 100 g of powder means 26 % m/m.

Tap each line of the table and watch how much of the 100 g it fills.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two labels that both say 5 %

Glucose solution (an infusion, given through a drip) is a solid dissolved in a liquid. Its label says 5 % w/v, so there are **5 g of glucose in every 100 mL** of solution.

Vinegar is a liquid dissolved in a liquid. Its label says 5 % v/v, so there are **5 mL of acetic acid in every 100 mL** of solution.

The number is the same, but it does not mean the same thing. Choose what each 5 is, grams or millilitres.

**Read the letters after the % sign before you trust the number.**

## A bigger bottle, the same percentage

Many people expect a bigger bottle to have a bigger percentage. First guess what the label on a 1000 mL glucose bottle will say. Then change the size.

The label stays at 5 %, because every 100 mL still holds 5 g. But the 1000 mL bottle holds 50 g of glucose in all, and the 500 mL bottle holds 25 g.

**A percentage tells you how strong a solution is, not how much of it you have.**

## Four questions for any label

Ask these whenever you read a percentage.

- **Which letters?** m/m (or w/w), m/v (or w/v), or v/v.
- **Out of what?** 100 g or 100 mL of the solution, never of the solvent alone.
- **How big is the packet?** Multiply to find the total amount.
- **Is 5 % always 5 g?** No: only the letters can tell you.
