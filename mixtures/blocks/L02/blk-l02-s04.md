# The Proportion That Saved Lives

<!-- STEP -->
## 1971: the drip bottles ran out

In 1971, during the war for Bangladesh, millions of refugees crossed into India. In the camps at Bangaon in West Bengal, cholera spread quickly. Cholera drains water and salts from the body, and a patient can die within hours.

The usual treatment was salt solution through a drip, and the camps did not have enough.

Dr Dilip Mahalanabis, an Indian children's doctor, tried something else: families gave patients a measured solution of salt and sugar by mouth.

Deaths in his camp fell about eight times, from about 30 in every 100 patients to fewer than 4. The World Health Organization later spread this treatment across the world, and Oral Rehydration Solution, or ORS, has saved millions of lives.

## Why the amounts matter

The sugar in ORS is not there for taste: it helps the gut take in the salt, and water follows the salt into the body.

Each ORS packet is made for one fixed volume of water. Try the jug. With half the water, or a second packet, you still get a solution of salt and sugar. But it is too strong to be ORS.

That is also why not all sugary drinks, made at home or sold in the market, are ORS.

**Salt, sugar and water must be in the right proportion. Otherwise it is not ORS.**
