# Where Did the 46 Grams Go?

<!-- STEP -->
## From 60 °C down to 40 °C

Take a saturated solution of compound B at 60 °C: 287 g dissolved in 100 g of water.

Now cool it slowly to 40 °C. The curve says only 241 g can stay dissolved at 40 °C.

287 g minus 241 g is 46 g. So what happens to those 46 g?

<!-- STEP -->
## It comes out as a solid

The water at 40 °C cannot hold 287 g, so 46 g separates out as a solid. It settles on the bottom of the beaker, often as crystals.

You might think the 46 g has left, but nothing has been taken out. The compound is still in the beaker. It is just no longer dissolved.

**Cooling a saturated solution turns some of the dissolved solute back into a solid.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Can we use this on purpose?

Warm the beaker back to 60 °C and the solid dissolves again.

So temperature can move a solute out of a solution and back in. Can we use this on purpose, to get a pure solid out of a mixture? That is the next lesson.
