# A Beaker That Is Only Cooled

<!-- STEP -->
## Cool it yourself

At the end of the last lesson, compound B came out of its solution when the water cooled. Now you can do that with your own hand.

This beaker holds 100 g of water at 60 °C with **287 g of compound B** dissolved in it. The solution is saturated, but it is still completely clear.

Bring the temperature down to **40 °C** and watch the floor of the beaker.

<!-- STEP -->
## A solid appears in a clear liquid

Grains form and settle on the bottom, and the reading for what is still dissolved falls to 241 g.

Now raise the temperature to 80 °C and the solid dissolves again. Bring it slowly down to 20 °C, and more solid comes out than before.

You put nothing into the beaker and took nothing out of it, so **temperature is the only thing you changed.**

## A way to get a solid back

A dissolved solid is hard to get at. You cannot see it, and you cannot pick it up or pour it off. Cooling turned it back into something you can lift out.

So cooling can be used on purpose, to get a solid back from its solution. That raises two questions: how much solid comes out, and is that solid pure? We will work out how much first.
