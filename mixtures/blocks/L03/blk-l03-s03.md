# 115 Grams Come Out

<!-- STEP -->
## Subtract the two numbers

The beaker holds 320 g of compound B. At 20 °C, the water can keep only 205 g dissolved, so the rest must come out as a solid.

**320 g - 205 g = 115 g**

If you picked 205 g, you read the curve correctly but answered a different question. That number is what stays in the water, and 115 g is what comes out.

<!-- STEP -->
## Where did the solid come from?

It is reasonable to think something new has appeared, or that the missing compound has left the beaker as vapour. Neither happened, because this is the same compound B that went in, and all 320 g is still in the beaker.

What changed is its solubility. Water at 20 °C can hold less compound B than water at 70 °C, so 115 g stopped being dissolved.

**Nothing left the beaker. Part of the compound moved from dissolved to solid.**

<!-- STEP -->
## What a crystal is

The solid often comes out as crystals. A **crystal** is a solid made up of particles arranged in a regular geometric pattern. Rock salt and mishri are both crystals.

Forming crystals from a saturated solution is called **crystallization**.

## Is the solid pure?

Your textbook says the solid comes out as a pure solid. This beaker held only one compound, so that is no surprise.
