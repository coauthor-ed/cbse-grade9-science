# Salt From the Sea

<!-- STEP -->
## Only the water leaves

Salt from the sea is not made by cooling. People use a shallow pan and the sun.

The sun and the wind turn water into vapour, and the level in the pan falls. Salt does not evaporate, so all of it stays behind in less and less water. Try the sun and wind, and watch how long the liquid stays clear.

Seawater holds so little salt that about nine-tenths of the water must leave before the solution is saturated.

<!-- STEP -->
## Evaporation and crystallization are different

These two words are easy to mix up, because in a salt pan they happen together. They are different processes.

- **Evaporation** removes the solvent. Here, water leaves as vapour.
- **Crystallization** is the solid forming, once the solution has become saturated.

So the pan goes through three stages: seawater, then a saturated solution, then salt crystals. Evaporation carries the pan from the first stage to the second, and crystals form after that.

**Evaporation takes the water away. Crystallization is what happens to the salt when too little water is left to hold it.**

## Two old Indian ways of making salt

Communities on India's coasts made salt long before modern laboratories. **Panga salt** was made by boiling concentrated sea brine, so the water left fast. **Karkatch salt** was made by evaporating sea water in the open, so the water left slowly.

The two methods gave salt crystals of different sizes. The reason is the same one you met with copper sulfate: the speed of the process changes the size of the crystals.
