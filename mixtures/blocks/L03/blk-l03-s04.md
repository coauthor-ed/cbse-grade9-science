# Growing Copper Sulfate Crystals

<!-- STEP -->
## Step 1: Make a saturated solution

Copper sulfate is toxic, so work with an adult beside you and do not touch it with bare hands. Common salt also works if you have no copper sulfate. But salt changes little with temperature, so it gives few crystals on cooling: leave its solution to evaporate slowly instead.

Put 1 g of copper sulfate and 25 mL of water in a beaker. Your teacher adds **one drop of dilute sulfuric acid**, handled very carefully, which helps to give pure crystals by preventing unwanted reactions.

Heat the beaker gently in a water bath and stir. Keep adding copper sulfate until no more dissolves.

**A solution is saturated when it cannot dissolve any more solute at that temperature.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Step 2: Filter while it is hot

Pour the hot solution through filter paper into a clean beaker. Anything that does not dissolve stays on the paper, so insoluble impurities are removed here.

The solution must be hot for this step. If it cools in the funnel, crystals start to form on the paper, and you lose your copper sulfate along with the dirt.

Cover the clean beaker with a watch glass to keep dust out.

<!-- STEP -->
## Step 3: Leave it to cool

Now leave the beaker alone and do not disturb it. As the solution cools, it can hold less copper sulfate, so crystals grow on the floor of the beaker.

Cooling slowly gives the particles in the solution time to come together. That gives large, shiny, well-shaped blue crystals.

<!-- STEP -->
## Slow cooling and fast cooling

You might expect fast cooling to give the same crystals sooner. Try both and compare them.

Split a hot solution into two equal parts. Leave one at room temperature and stand the other in ice-cold water. The second part cools fast, and it gives many small, poorly shaped crystals.

The two beakers hold the same compound, in about the same amount. **The speed of cooling changes the size and shape of the crystals.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Step 4: Filter, rinse and dry

Filter the crystals out of the liquid. Rinse them with a little cold water, because cold water dissolves less of them than warm water would. The rinse carries away the solution left on their surface.

Then let the crystals dry on a watch glass.

**The four steps are: saturate, filter hot, cool slowly, then filter, rinse and dry.**
