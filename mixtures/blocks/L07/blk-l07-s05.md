# Making Tiny Grains Clump Together

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the grains stay up

Spinning works well for a small tube, but a whole tank of muddy water will not fit in a centrifuge. Its finest grains also pass through filter paper and hardly sink.

They stay apart for a second reason. The idea of electric charge goes beyond your textbook, but it explains why alum works. Each tiny grain carries the same small electric charge, so the grains push one another away and never join up. Push two of them together and watch the lens.

<!-- STEP -->
## A spoon of alum

**Alum** (*fitkari*) is a white crystalline substance that is often used to clean water. Powdered alum dissolves when you stir it in, and at first you will see no change.

The alum cancels the charge on the grains. They stop pushing one another away, so they touch, stick and grow into clumps that you can see. This process is called **coagulation**, and the alum is called a **coagulant**.

<!-- STEP -->
## The clumps sink

A clump is much bigger than one grain, so it sinks much faster. The clumps settle to the bottom by gravity, which is called **sedimentation**.

Clear water is left on top. You can pour it off, which is decantation, or you can filter it, because the clumps are now too large to pass through the paper.

**Coagulation makes tiny particles big enough to sink and to be filtered.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Too little, too much

You might think that more alum always works better, but it does not. With too little alum, only some of the charge is cancelled, so few clumps form.

With too much, the grains pick up the opposite charge (this also goes beyond your textbook). They start pushing one another away again, and the water stays cloudy. This is why a water plant tests the dose first. Try both ends of the dial.

<!-- STEP -->
## Paneer is coagulation too

Add lemon juice or vinegar to hot milk, and the milk proteins form clumps. The acid is the coagulant here, and the clumps are paneer. Blood clotting at a cut is another kind of coagulation.

So you now have two ways to clear a cloudy liquid. **Spinning pushes the particles harder. Coagulation makes the particles bigger.**
