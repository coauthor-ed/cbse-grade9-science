# Stirred, but Not Dissolved

<!-- STEP -->
## Sand in a jar of water

Stir sand into a jar of water as hard as you like. The grains go round and round, and you can still see every one of them. In a solution you cannot see the solute at all, so this is something different.

A heterogeneous mixture whose solid particles do not dissolve, but stay spread through the liquid, is called a **suspension**. Its particles are much larger than those in a solution, and you can see them. Sawdust in water and tea leaves in water are suspensions too, as was the chalk in beaker B.

Pick a jar and stir it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Leave muddy water alone

Now try muddy water. You might expect all the mud to sink if you only wait long enough. Move the clock forward and watch what happens.

The heavier mud particles settle at the bottom, but the water above can stay cloudy. The finest grains are so small that they sink very slowly, and there is still mud in that cloudy water.

**Settling takes out the heavier particles, and the finest ones stay up.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Try a filter

The next idea is a good one: pour the cloudy water through filter paper. Some larger particles stay on the paper, but the water that comes through is often still cloudy. This shows that filtering is not always enough to separate tiny particles.

Two other methods can do the job. **Centrifugation** spins the tube very fast, and **coagulation** makes the tiny particles stick together into clumps. We start with the spinning.
