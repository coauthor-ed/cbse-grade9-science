# The Other Two Tests

<!-- STEP -->
## Leave them alone

Press the clock and leave the three beakers undisturbed for ten minutes.

B has changed. The chalk has sunk to the bottom as a pale layer, and the liquid above it is much clearer.

A has not changed. C has not changed either: it is just as cloudy from top to bottom, and no layer has formed.

**Time separates B, but it does nothing to C.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Now the filter paper

Tap a beaker, then pour it through a cone of filter paper. Try all three.

- **A** runs through and stays clear. Nothing is left on the paper.
- **B** leaves a white layer of chalk on the paper, and clear liquid comes through.
- **C** runs through and is still cloudy. Nothing is left on the paper.

You may have expected a cloudy liquid to leave something behind. C does not, because its particles are small enough to pass through the holes in the paper.

<!-- STEP -->
## All three tests together

| Beaker | Beam | After 10 minutes | Filter paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** salt and water | no path | no change | nothing left |
| **B** chalk and water | path seen | settles | chalk left |
| **C** milk and water | path seen | no change | nothing left |

B and C looked alike, but they behave differently. C scatters light like B, yet it does not settle or stay on the paper.

So this bench holds three kinds of mixture. A is a solution, and later lessons name the other two.
