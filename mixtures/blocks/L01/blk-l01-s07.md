# Two Solutions of Salt

<!-- STEP -->
## Both are solutions

Two containers now stand on the bench.

- **The beaker:** 10 g of common salt, about a big spoonful, in 100 mL of solution.
- **The bottle:** 0.9 g of common salt in 100 mL of solution. This is the saline that goes into a patient's vein.

Both show no beam path. Neither changes on the clock, and neither leaves anything on the filter paper.

**All three tests say the same thing: both are solutions.**

<!-- STEP -->
## What the tests cannot tell you

These two liquids are very different. One is safe for a patient's blood, and the other is much too salty.

The tests tell you that a mixture is a solution. They say nothing about how much salt is in it, and that amount is what matters here.

In the next lesson you will learn how to say exactly how much is dissolved.
