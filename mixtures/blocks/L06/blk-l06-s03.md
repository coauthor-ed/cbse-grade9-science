# Drawing Off One Layer at a Time

<!-- STEP -->
## The water comes out first

The stopcock is at the bottom, and the water is the layer sitting on it, so the water leaves first.

The layers form in this order because of **density**, which is the mass of a fixed volume of a substance. Water is denser than mustard oil, so it sinks and the oil floats. The two do not mix, so each one stays in its own layer.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the oil does not lead

Many students say the oil comes first because it is on top. That is true of a jug, which you tip so that the top layer pours out. A funnel is never tipped: liquid leaves through a hole at the bottom, so the top layer comes out last.

Take the glass stopper out before you open the stopcock. As liquid leaves, air has to come in at the top to fill the space. With the stopper in, air cannot enter and the flow soon stops.

<!-- STEP -->
## Stop before the line

Close the stopcock when the water is almost fully drained. The next small portion may contain both liquids, so collect it in a separate container and throw it away.

Then open the stopcock again and collect the oil in a clean flask. You lose a few drops, and in return you have two separate liquids.

<!-- STEP -->
## Immiscible and miscible

Liquids that do not mix, such as mustard oil and water, are called **immiscible**. This word is new. You met its opposite in lesson 3: liquids that do mix are **miscible**, such as water and alcohol, water and vinegar, acetone and water.

A funnel works only on immiscible liquids, because miscible liquids have no layers to draw off. For those you need the distillation of lesson 4.

<!-- STEP -->
## What if the densities are the same?

Your textbook asks a good question here. Suppose two immiscible liquids have the same density. Neither one sinks and neither one floats, so no neat layers form. One liquid just hangs as blobs in the other, wherever it was poured.

There is no lower layer for the stopcock to release.

**The funnel uses a difference in density. With no difference, it has nothing to work with.**
