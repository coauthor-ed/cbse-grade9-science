# Two Liquids That Do Not Mix

<!-- STEP -->
## Oil meets water

Have you seen what happens when water falls into a pan that has oil in it? Try it on the bench: pour mustard oil into a glass of water.

Salt and sugar disappeared into water, so you might expect the oil to do the same. Instead it rises and collects in a layer on top, with one sharp line between the two liquids.

<!-- STEP -->
## Shake the glass

Put a lid on the glass and shake it hard. The whole glass turns cloudy gold, and now it does look mixed.

Stop shaking and keep watching. The oil was only broken into tiny drops, and the drops rise and join up again until the two layers are back.

**Shaking breaks the oil into small drops. It does not make oil and water mix.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Which liquid goes on top?

Try two other liquids with the water. Kerosene also floats, while chloroform sinks to the bottom and the water sits above it.

Each pair gives two layers and one sharp line, and the order never changes for that pair. Since the two liquids are already apart, you do not need heat to separate them. You only need a way to draw off one layer without disturbing the other.
