# Brass: A Mixture That Looks Like One Metal

<!-- STEP -->
## Three metals on the bench

Each of these looks like one solid metal, but each is a mixture:

- **Brass:** about 80 % copper and 20 % zinc
- **Bronze:** about 80 % copper and 20 % tin
- **Stainless steel:** iron with chromium (16 to 18 %), nickel (10 to 14 %), molybdenum (2 to 3 %) and a little carbon (0.03 to 0.8 %)

A homogeneous mixture of two or more metals, or of a metal and a non-metal, is called an **alloy**.

<!-- STEP -->
## Made by melting

Metals do not dissolve into each other at room temperature. When they are melted at high temperatures, they can be mixed to form a solution. As this hot mixture cools, it becomes a solid that looks like a single metal.

Alloys are made because the mixture is better for the job than the pure metals: stronger, more rigid, or more resistant to corrosion.

<!-- STEP -->
## Try each method

First guess which method will separate brass, then try each one on each metal:

- **Filter:** a filter stops solid pieces in a liquid. An alloy has no liquid and no separate pieces.
- **Magnet:** copper, zinc and tin are not attracted by a magnet. Stainless steel contains iron, but the iron is mixed all through it, so no iron comes away alone.
- **Separating funnel:** a funnel needs one layer above another. An alloy is the same from top to bottom.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why nothing works

An alloy is a solid solution: the metals are mixed evenly all the way through. Each method needs a difference between parts that are apart from one another, and here there are no such parts.

**Physical methods cannot separate the components of an alloy.**

A chemical reaction can remove one metal: dilute acid reacts with the zinc in brass and leaves the copper. But that makes a new substance, so it is not a physical separation.
