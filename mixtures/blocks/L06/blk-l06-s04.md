# Mixtures With a Gas, Mixtures of Solids

<!-- STEP -->
## Gases mix evenly

Gas particles are free to move in all directions, so one gas mixes easily and evenly with another. This is why most mixtures of gases are homogeneous.

Hydrogen and oxygen, used together as rocket fuel, are one example, and air is another. Lift the divider and watch two gases spread through each other. No layers form.

<!-- STEP -->
## Smoke, fog and dust

Some mixtures with a gas in them are not even. **Smoke** has solid particles in air, **fog** has tiny water droplets in air, and **dust in the air** has solid specks in it.

These bits are far bigger than gas particles, so they are not spread evenly, and they can often be seen. These three are heterogeneous mixtures.

Sort the five samples: which ones are homogeneous, and which are not?

## Two solids

Most mixtures of two solids are heterogeneous, and no liquid is there to help. So look for a property that only one of the solids has.

You may have used a magnet to pull iron nails out of sawdust in Class 6. Iron is attracted to a magnet and sawdust is not, so the nails come up and the sawdust stays behind.

**The difference used here is magnetism.**
