# Distillation in Kannauj

<!-- STEP -->
## The smell of the first rain

You must have noticed the pleasant earthy smell that rises from the ground after the first rain. In **Kannauj**, a town in Uttar Pradesh known as the perfume capital of India, that smell is captured in a natural perfume called **Mitti ka Ittar**.

It is made by a traditional distillation method passed down through generations, called the **Deg-Bhapka method**.

<!-- STEP -->
## The same jobs in copper and bamboo

There is no thermometer here. The other parts of your set-up all have a match.

- **Deg:** a large copper vessel holding water with flowers or other fragrant material, sealed with clay over a fire. This is the distillation flask.
- **Chonga:** a bamboo pipe that carries the vapour away. This is the side arm.
- **Bhapka:** a copper receiver standing in a trough of cold water. This is the condenser and the collecting flask together.

Fragrant oils leave the deg with the steam, and they condense in the cool bhapka.

<!-- STEP -->
## Keep the fire gentle

Now make the fire bigger. It seems sensible that more heat should give more perfume. It gives less, because vapour reaches the bhapka faster than the cold water can cool it, so some escapes into the air with its scent.

Lower the fire, and the vapour comes slowly enough to condense.

**A condenser can only cool vapour so fast. A fire that is too big loses the product.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The same idea today

Apart from the thermometer, the parts match the set-up you have just used, and so does the idea: the vapour leaves, then something cold turns it back into liquid.

The Fragrance and Flavour Development Centre in Kannauj now separates flavour and fragrance components from flowers, leaves and other plant parts. It also helps farmers who grow fragrance plants and people who want to start perfume businesses.
