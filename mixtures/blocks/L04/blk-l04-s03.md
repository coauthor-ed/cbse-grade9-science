# The Distillation Set-Up

<!-- STEP -->
## Four jobs: boil, measure, cool, collect

Here is the set-up from your textbook, with a mixture of acetone and water in the flask.

- **Boil:** a burner heats the distillation flask, which stands on a wire gauze and a tripod stand.
- **Measure:** a thermometer goes through the top of the flask.
- **Cool:** the vapour passes down the inner tube of a water condenser, with cold water around it.
- **Collect:** the liquid runs into a conical flask.

<!-- STEP -->
## Water goes in at the bottom

The condenser jacket has two openings. Cold water goes in at the lower one and comes out at the upper one.

The reason is simple: water fed in at the bottom fills the jacket as it rises, so the whole tube stays cooled. Now turn the flow round. Water fed in at the top runs straight out of the lower opening and air stays in the jacket. Much of the tube is then not cooled, and some vapour gets through.

**Water in at the bottom keeps the jacket full.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The thermometer bulb sits in the vapour

You might expect the bulb to go down into the boiling liquid. It does not. It sits in the vapour, level with the opening of the side arm.

We put it there because we need the temperature of what is leaving the flask, and that is what goes on to become the distillate.

Now move the bulb up the neck. The vapour leaves through the side arm, so little of it reaches the bulb, and the reading is too low. A student who trusts that reading may turn up the heat when nothing is wrong.

**The bulb goes level with the side arm, in the vapour that is leaving.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Run it

Run the distillation. The reading rises to about 56 °C, the boiling point of acetone, and stays there while clear liquid drips into the conical flask.

Water boils at 100 °C, so at this temperature very little of it turns to vapour. It stays in the flask, which holds less and less acetone as the run goes on.

Acetone and water are 44 °C apart, which is why the separation works so well.

<!-- STEP -->
## Salt water in the same set-up

Now put salt water in the flask. The reading rises to 100 °C, the boiling point of water, and pure water drips into the conical flask.

Salt does not turn to vapour at this temperature, so all of it stays behind. This is the answer to the question at the end of the last lesson.

**Distillation gets pure water back from salt water. The solid stays in the flask.**
