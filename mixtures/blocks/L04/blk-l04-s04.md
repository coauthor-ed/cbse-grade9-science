# How Far Apart Must Two Boiling Points Be?

<!-- STEP -->
## Five liquids on one thermometer

Here are the five liquids from your textbook, marked at their boiling points.

- Acetone 56 °C
- Chloroform 61 °C
- Alcohol 78 °C
- Benzene 80 °C
- Water 100 °C

Tap any two to see how far apart they are. A difference between the parts is what every separation uses, and here that difference is the boiling point.

<!-- STEP -->
## About 25 °C apart

Your textbook gives the rule: simple distillation separates liquids that differ in boiling point by **at least about 25 °C**.

With a big gap, one liquid turns to vapour before the vapour of the other forms in any large amount. Acetone and water are 44 °C apart, so nearly pure acetone comes over first.

With a small gap, the vapour holds a lot of both. Alcohol and benzene are only 2 °C apart, so what comes over is still a mixture.

<!-- STEP -->
## Near the limit, and pairs that do not mix

The rule says "about", so it is not an exact limit. Acetone and benzene are 24 °C apart, which is near the limit: expect a poor separation, not a clean one.

Two pairs need a different check first. Chloroform and benzene do not mix with water at all, so they form two layers. You would not distil those. You will separate them in lesson 6 with a funnel.

**Check first that the liquids mix. Then check that their boiling points are about 25 °C apart or more.**
