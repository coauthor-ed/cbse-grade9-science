# Why the Colours Do Not Stay Together

<!-- STEP -->
## Same start, different distances

All three colours start from the same pencil line at the same moment. After a while the yellow is high up the strip, the pink is in the middle and the blue has moved the least.

Run the same ink with the same paper and water, and you get the same order every time. So the order is not an accident: something about each dye decides how far it goes.

<!-- STEP -->
## Two things act on each dye

Two things act on a dye at the same time. The moving water dissolves it and carries it up, and the paper holds on to it.

- A dye that dissolves easily and sticks only weakly to the paper travels far.
- A dye that dissolves less easily and sticks strongly to the paper stays behind.

Think of three friends walking past a row of shops at the same speed. One stops at every shop, one at a few, and one at none, so they soon end up far apart. The paper plays the part of the shops.

**The difference used here is how far each substance travels on paper.**

<!-- STEP -->
## A name in two pieces

Greek names can look hard, and nobody is born knowing them. This one is built from two short pieces:

- **chroma** means colour
- **graphein** means to write

Look at the strip again. The pen made one black spot, and the water has spread it out as a line of separate colours. What would you call that, using those two pieces?

<!-- STEP -->
## Chromatography

The word is **chromatography**, which means writing with colour. It got this name because it was first used to separate coloured substances such as dyes and inks.

Done on paper, the method is called **paper chromatography**. Special chromatographic paper is sold for it, but a strip of filter paper also works.
