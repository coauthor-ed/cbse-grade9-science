# A Drop of Water on a Black Line

<!-- STEP -->
## One black line

Take a black sketch pen and draw a thick line on a piece of paper. The line looks like one colour, so you would guess that the ink in the pen is one substance.

There is an easy way to test that guess. Put one drop of water on the line, then watch the outer edge of the wet patch.

<!-- STEP -->
## The drop spreads

The water soaks outward through the paper and takes the ink along with it. Where the drop landed, the line goes pale.

Now look at the edge of the wet patch. It is not black. Rings of colour have appeared there, and each colour sits at its own distance from the centre.

**A black line gave colours, and nobody added any colour to the paper.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Where did the colours come from?

Perhaps the water or the paper made them. Test that by putting the same water on a clean part of the paper: the wet patch has no colour at all, so the colours came from the ink.

You might also think the paper worked like a filter. That is a reasonable idea, but a filter holds back solid pieces, and here the colours moved along with the water, dissolved in it.

**Black ink is a mixture of several coloured substances, called dyes.**

A wet patch shows this only roughly. A strip of paper standing in water pulls the colours fully apart, and we set one up next.
