# Setting Up the Strip

<!-- STEP -->
## What stands in the jar

Four things matter in this set-up:

- the **paper strip**, 3 cm wide, with its lower end in the water
- the **pencil line**, 2 cm from the bottom
- the **ink spot**, in the middle of the line
- the **water**, a thin layer at the bottom of the jar

The line is drawn in pencil because pencil marks do not dissolve in water. A line drawn with a pen would run up the paper along with the ink you want to watch.

<!-- STEP -->
## The water stays below the spot

The water level must start below the spot. It is reasonable to think the water has to touch the ink from the start, and it will touch it, but only by climbing the paper.

Raise the level above the spot and see what goes wrong. The ink dissolves straight into the water in the jar, so the water turns faintly coloured and the strip is left bare.

**Keep the spot above the water at the start, or the ink ends up in the jar.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The water climbs

With the level below the spot, press Run. Water soaks up through the paper, reaches the spot and dissolves the ink. As the water keeps rising it carries the ink up with it.

The ink does not rise as one mark. It opens into separate colour spots, because the colours do not all move at the same speed.

<!-- STEP -->
## This is not filtering

Many students still think of a filter here, because a paper is involved. A filter stops solid pieces and lets the liquid through.

On this strip nothing is stopped. Every colour is dissolved in the moving water and every colour moves, but some move further than others.

**The strip separates dissolved colours from one another.**
