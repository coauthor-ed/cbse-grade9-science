# Five Samples and Four Bottles

<!-- STEP -->
## Five samples

Paper chromatography works on many coloured mixtures, not only ink. Here are five samples you can try, and each one holds more than one dye:

- **black sketch-pen ink**
- **green food colour**
- **green extract of spinach leaves**
- **colour from marigold petals**
- **coloured fennel seeds**, the mouth freshener served after a meal

Each of these looks like one colour, and the strip shows what is hiding in it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Four bottles

The liquid that climbs the paper is the solvent, and you have four to choose from:

- **water**
- **alcohol**
- **a mixture of solvents**
- **2 % m/v salt solution**, which your book suggests for green food colour

Pick a sample and a bottle, then press Run. Start with water on the ink and the green food colour, where it works well.

<!-- STEP -->
## Where water does not work

Now try water on the spinach extract. The water climbs the strip all the way, but the green stays on the pencil line. The same thing happens with the marigold colour.

You might think the run needs more time, but the water has already passed the spot. These dyes do not dissolve in water, so water has nothing to carry. Alcohol does dissolve them, and in alcohol the spot opens into separate colours.

<!-- STEP -->
## Will water work every time?

Your textbook asks this question directly, and the bench has now answered it: no. In some cases you need a different solvent, such as alcohol or a mixture of solvents.

**A solvent can only carry what it dissolves, so choose a solvent that dissolves the sample.**
