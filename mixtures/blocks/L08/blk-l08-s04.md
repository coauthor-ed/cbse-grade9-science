# Fill In the Table Yourself

## An empty table

Your textbook gives you a table to complete. It has three columns, solution, suspension and colloid, and six rows: nature, particle size, visibility, filtration, settling and the Tyndall effect.

Run each test on beakers A, B and C to fill the rows. Before each test, guess what the milk will do, because milk is the column that surprises most people.

<!-- STEP -->
## Row by row

- **Nature.** A is homogeneous. B and C are heterogeneous.
- **Particle size.** Less than 1 nm, more than 1000 nm, and 1 to 1000 nm.
- **Visibility.** A's particles cannot be seen. B's can be seen with your eyes. C's cannot be seen with your eyes.
- **Filtration.** A and C pass through the paper. B stays on the paper.
- **Settling.** Only B settles.
- **Tyndall effect.** B and C show the beam. A does not.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why milk goes through the paper

White and cloudy-looking milk seems like something a filter should stop. But the milk comes through the paper as white as it went in, just as the salt solution comes through.

Filter paper stops only particles larger than its gaps, and the particles in milk are smaller than those gaps. The tiny grains in muddy water passed through for the same reason. Being cloudy does not mean that something can be filtered out.

<!-- STEP -->
## One number explains the table

Read the table from left to right by size. The smallest particles do not scatter light, settle or stay on paper. The largest do all three. The middle size scatters light, but it does not settle and it does not stay on paper.

**Particle size explains every row of the table.**
