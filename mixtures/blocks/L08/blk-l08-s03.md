# The Beam Test, Named at Last

<!-- STEP -->
## Back to the laser

Go back to the three beakers from the first lesson and pass the thin beam through each one.

- **A**, salt solution: you cannot see the path of the beam.
- **B**, chalk in water: a bright line crosses the liquid.
- **C**, milk: a bright line again, though the mixture looks uniform.

**C looks like a solution, but it treats light like a suspension.**

<!-- STEP -->
## What the particles do to the light

A beam goes straight ahead, so from the side none of its light reaches your eye. The particles in B and C scatter (spread) some of the light in all directions, and some of that reaches you.

The particles in milk are much smaller than chalk grains, but there are very many of them. The scattering of light by particles is called the **Tyndall effect**, after the scientist John Tyndall, who first explained it.

<!-- STEP -->
## A dark room

You can see the same thing without a beaker. A thin beam of sunlight enters a dark room through a small hole, and you can see its whole path.

Air alone would not show this. Dust and smoke particles in the air scatter the light, and that scattered light is what you see.

<!-- STEP -->
## A stadium at night

Floodlights in a sports stadium show long, bright cones of light in the night air. You can trace them from the lamps down towards the ground.

This is the same effect on a bigger scale: dust and tiny drops in the air scatter the light. Light always travels from the lamps to the field, but you see its path only when something scatters it.

<!-- STEP -->
## Clouds and hazy cities

Light is scattered when it passes through a colloid or a suspension, but not when it passes through a transparent solution.

Clouds are tiny water drops or ice crystals floating in air. They stay spread out and they scatter light, so they behave as a colloid. Cities with a lot of smoke and dust often look hazy for the same reason. The particles scatter light in all directions, so distant buildings look pale.

**A path of light shows that particles are there. A solution shows no path.**
