# From Green Cane to White Sugar

<!-- STEP -->
## The question on the first page

Your textbook opens with this question: how do we get sweet, white crystals from tall, green sugarcane? You can work it out now.

The sugar is dissolved in the juice, and the juice is held inside the tough fibres of the cane. Tap the steps in the order you would do them: **crush, filter, evaporate, crystallize, spin**. If a step cannot work yet, you will see why.

<!-- STEP -->
## The difference behind each step

Crushing only presses out the juice. After that, each step uses a difference, so name it.

- **Filter:** fibre and mud are bigger than the gaps, so the difference is size.
- **Evaporate:** water leaves as vapour and sugar stays, so the difference is boiling point.
- **Crystallize:** when the syrup holds more sugar than the water can keep dissolved, crystals form, so the difference is solubility.
- **Spin:** a sugar mill's centrifuge is a drum with tiny holes. The syrup is thrown out through the holes and the crystals are too big to follow, so this spin uses size, not density.

Real mills add more cleaning steps to take out the colour.

## Four more questions you can answer

The same page asked other questions, and you can now answer them all. Pick one to see it.

**Why does mud settle, but not milk?** Mud grains are larger than 1000 nm, so they sink. Milk is a colloid: its particles are too small to settle, and they stay spread through the liquid.

**How is evaporation different from boiling?** You met this in the lesson on distillation. Evaporation happens only at the surface of a liquid, at any temperature. Boiling happens all through the liquid, at its boiling point, with bubbles of vapour.

**Why do we see rays of sunlight between the leaves of a tree?** Dust and tiny drops in the air scatter the light towards your eye. This is the Tyndall effect.

**How do doctors find malaria in a few drops of blood?** A spin gathers the infected cells in one place, so they are easier to find under a microscope.
