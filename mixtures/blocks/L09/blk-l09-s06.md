## From a bench to a town

Every method so far has fitted on a bench. Now think of the dirty water that leaves all the homes in a town. This **sewage** carries heavy solids and very fine particles.

Sewage must be treated before it is released into the environment. A sewage plant uses three methods that you know, one after another: **sedimentation**, **coagulation** and **filtration**.

Run the first stage and watch the colour of the water.

<!-- STEP -->
## What each stage does

- **Sedimentation.** The water stands still in a wide tank. Heavier solids sink to the bottom, so the difference used is density.
- **Coagulation.** A coagulant such as alum makes the fine particles clump, and the clumps sink too. You did this in a jar in the lesson on suspensions.
- **Filtration.** The water passes down through layers of sand and gravel, which hold back the small bits that are left. The difference used is size.

<!-- STEP -->
## Why this order?

You might think the plant could start with the filter, since that is the part that looks cleanest. Try it. The heavy solids reach the sand first and block it, and the water stops flowing.

Sedimentation takes the big load away first, and coagulation turns the fine particles into clumps that can sink.

**Each stage gives the next stage an easier job.**

## Where the clean water goes

This water is not for drinking. It is clean enough to be reused for flushing toilets or watering plants, and it can be released safely into the environment.

Every bucket used in this way is a bucket of fresh water that nobody had to take from a river or a well. Choose where the water goes.

## Separation all around you

Separation also happens in your body, because your kidneys remove waste from your blood.

It starts at home too. **Dry waste**, such as plastic, paper, glass and metal, can be recycled. **Wet waste**, such as food scraps and vegetable peels, can be composted. Sort the items here.

Researchers are finding ways to recover lithium from old phone and laptop batteries. We also have to remove plastic waste from oceans and rivers.

**Better ways of separating waste and recycling materials can make the world cleaner, healthier and more sustainable.**
