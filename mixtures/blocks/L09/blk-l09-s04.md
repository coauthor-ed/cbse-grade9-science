# Challenge: Three Solids in One Dish

<!-- STEP -->
## What is in the dish

A china dish holds three dry solids mixed together: **sand**, **common salt** and **naphthalene**. This mixture comes from your textbook, and it asks for the correct order of methods.

First name the difference that will take out each solid. Then put the steps in order, and run your plan one step at a time.

**You win when all three solids are back, dry and on their own.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why the order matters

Each step is right for one of the solids, so the only hard part is when to use it.

- **Naphthalene:** taking it out first is simplest, while the dish is dry. It does not dissolve in water, and once it is wet with the sand you cannot sublime it cleanly.
- **Salt** needs water, because water is what parts it from the sand.
- **Sand** stays on the filter paper.

<!-- STEP -->
## If your order is wrong

The bench shows what went wrong, and you can put the solids back and plan again. A filter on a dry powder separates nothing. If you boil before you filter, the salt comes back mixed with the sand.

The second round adds iron filings and a magnet. There is no clock, and a wrong plan costs you nothing.
