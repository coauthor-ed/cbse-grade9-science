# Conservation Needs a Condition

## The working is right for an ideal fall

Set $mgh = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$ and you get $v = \sqrt{2gh}$. With no air, all the potential energy becomes kinetic energy by the ground.

## The word "exactly" is wrong

Conservation of mechanical energy holds only when no other force acts. A real ball in air feels air resistance all the way down.

Air resistance turns a little of the ball's energy into heat. So the real ball lands a little slower than $\sqrt{2gh}$, never faster.

## Two mistakes to avoid

- Gravity is not "another force" here. Potential energy $mgh$ already describes it.
- The mass is not missing: it cancels from both sides.
