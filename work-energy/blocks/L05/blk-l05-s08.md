# How High Can It Climb?

<!-- STEP -->
## A track you can reshape

This is a frictionless track and it is yours to drag. The panel beside it is the height-energy of the ball at every point along the way, so any hill you draw costs the ball some of its motion.

Release the ball at **O**. It sets off carrying everything it started with, and it can only spend that.

- It rolls down the first dip and gains speed.
- It climbs the hump after it, and pays for the climb in speed it gives back.
- Climb higher than it has, and it runs out **before** it gets to the top.

<!-- STEP -->
## It stops, and then it rolls back

Watch where it stops the first time. The height panel is sitting exactly on the level of where it started, and the speed panel has reached nothing at all.

**The ball can climb to its own starting height and not one centimetre higher.**

Now the same track, one hill taller.

The ball gets slower and slower going up, and the speed panel hits nothing while the ball is still well short of the top. It rolls back down.

<!-- STEP -->
## Which means something about the far end

The mark at the far end of the track is higher than the line the ball started on.

The ball will never touch it. Not slowly, not fast, not at any height setting. **It is not out of reach because of anything to do with the ball - it is out of reach because the ball does not have that much energy.**

Energy is not a vague quantity. It is a fixed amount, spent a little at a time, and every hill takes some of it.
