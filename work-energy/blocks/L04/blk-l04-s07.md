# Two Families, and One That Looks Like a Third

<!-- STEP -->
## The family, sorted

Five arrangements have now been banked in front of you, and they were not all banked the same way. The panel sorts them into two shelves.

**Shelf one: stored by deformation.** The slingshot band. The compressed spring. A bent bow, a wound watch spring, a loaded rubber tube. Something's shape is out of place, and it wants to go back.

**Shelf two: stored by separation.** The two magnets. The two charges. The ball above the ground. Nothing is bent - the parts are simply further apart than they want to be, and pulling them further costs more.

**The two shelves are the same idea at two scales, and the panel can move a card from one to the other so you can see it.**

## The dead end you are about to walk into

You have now watched five different things get banked, and every single one gave its energy back when released. Five for five.

It would be entirely reasonable to conclude that effort is banked. Push a heavy box across a rough floor - that is force, over a long distance, textbook work. Then stop. Surely that is one more deposit.

Here is the panel's third shelf, and it is deliberately nearly empty.

**Work done against friction stores nothing at all.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Why friction is the odd one out

The pattern across the first two shelves is that the force you pushed against is a force *between the parts of the system*. Magnets pull on magnets. A spring pushes back on whatever is compressing it. The Earth pulls on the ball.

Push a box along a rough floor and the friction is not pulling the box anywhere. It is pointing against the motion, whichever direction the motion is, and if you reversed the push it would point the other way too. There is no arrangement the box would return to. There is nothing to fall back towards.

Which is exactly why nothing comes back out.

**The energy you supplied went into heating the floor and the box, and sound, and it stays there. Same work done, completely different result.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The test you can carry

Here is the whole lesson in one question you can ask about any situation:

**Is there a position this thing would rather be in?**

- A bent bow would rather be straight. Yes - banked.
- Two magnets would rather be touching. Yes - banked.
- A book on a high shelf would rather be on the floor. Yes - banked.
- A box dragged across a rough floor has no arrangement to return to, and friction points against the push whichever way it goes. No - not stored.

**Potential energy needs somewhere to fall back towards. If there is no such place, the work is still work - it just is not stored.**
