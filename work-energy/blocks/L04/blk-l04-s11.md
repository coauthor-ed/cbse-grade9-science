# Challenge: Stack the Stores

<!-- STEP -->
## What is on the bench

A receiver with a dial showing how much energy it wants. Two ways to fill it.

- **A spring**, which you can compress. Compressing it stores energy, and it hands that energy back the moment you let it go.
- **A load on a lift**, which you can raise. Raising it stores energy because of the height, and the $g$ on that panel is the same $g$ you have been using all lesson.

Use the spring, use the lift, or use both. The target moves each round.

<!-- STEP -->
## There are two ways to land on the number, and only one of them is the expected one

The obvious route is the lift: raise the load until the energy it holds is the energy the receiver is asking for.

The other route is the spring from the start of the lesson. Compress the spring, let it go, and let the moving load carry the energy across instead. The spring's store and the lift's store are both real stores, and the receiver does not care which one paid.

**A run that combines them is not cheating. It is the panel asking whether you know there are two of them.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The one thing that will not work

There is a friction slider on that bench too. Set it and run, and the energy you put in goes into heating the track, and the receiver reads nothing.

That is not a bug in the panel. It is the dead end from earlier in this lesson, sitting right where you would try to reach for it.

**Every joule the receiver takes came out of a spring, a height, or both. There is no third way in, and there is no dial for the energy itself.**
