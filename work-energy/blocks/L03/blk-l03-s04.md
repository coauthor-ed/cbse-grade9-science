# K = ½mv²

<!-- STEP -->
## From work to a formula

Take an object of mass $m$. A steady force $F$ moves it through a displacement $s$. Its speed changes from $u$ to $v$, with acceleration $a$.

From earlier chapters, $F = ma$ and $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$, so $s = \dfrac{v^2 - u^2}{2a}$. Then

$$W = F \times s = ma \times \frac{v^2 - u^2}{2a} = \tfrac{1}{2}m(v^2 - u^2)$$

<!-- STEP -->
## Starting from rest

If the object starts at rest, $u = 0$. Its kinetic energy is

$$K = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$$

Here $m$ is the mass in kg and $v$ the speed in m/s, so $K$ is in joules. Check with the trolley: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 2 \times 3^2 = 9$ J.

<!-- STEP -->
## Speeding up and slowing down

Positive work speeds an object up, so its kinetic energy rises. Negative work slows it down, so its kinetic energy falls. No work: the kinetic energy stays the same.

Kinetic energy has no direction.
