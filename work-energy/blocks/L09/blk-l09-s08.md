# Build One, and Then Be Suspicious of It

<!-- STEP -->
## Put a machine together

There are parts on the bench: a bar, a rope, a wheel, a wedge, and a load to move. Build something with them and the panel will measure what you have made.

**Which of the three you build changes the number and nothing else.**

<!-- STEP -->
## Three questions worth more than the build

**Which machine suits holding a load steady rather than lifting it?** Holding a load does no work, because nothing moves, so any machine that lets a small force balance the load will do. Lifting needs work, and a machine cannot cut that.

**What measurement error would make your machine look better than it is?** A pivot that is not quite where you measured it, or a length rounded to a convenient number. Errors like these flatter the machine.

**Where did the energy that the machine did not hand back go?** Into friction, heat and sound. The shortfall is the most informative number on the panel.

<!-- STEP -->
## Compare input and output

Look at the work you put in and the work the load received. The load always gets a little less.

The difference is what the machine wasted, not what it created. Dividing the useful work out by the work in gives its **efficiency**: a machine that hands back 460 J for every 500 J has an efficiency of $460/500 = 92\%$.
