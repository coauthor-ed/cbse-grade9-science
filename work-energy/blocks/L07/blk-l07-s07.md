# Mechanical Advantage of a Ramp

<!-- STEP -->
## Work in, energy gained

A ramp is called an inclined plane. Push a load of mass $m$ up a smooth ramp of length $L$ to a height $h$, with an effort $F'$.

Your work, $F' \times L$, equals the potential energy gained, $mgh$. So $F' \times L = mgh$.

<!-- STEP -->
## L over h

**$MA = \dfrac{\text{load}}{\text{effort}} = \dfrac{mg}{F'} = \dfrac{L}{h}$.**

The longer the ramp for the same height, the smaller the effort.

<!-- STEP -->
## The work stays the same

The effort goes down, but the distance goes up. The work done stays $mgh$.
