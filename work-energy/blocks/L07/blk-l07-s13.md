# A Ruler, a Pencil, and Nothing Else

<!-- STEP -->
## The desk experiment

A ruler balanced on a pencil. A heavy thing on the short end of the ruler. Nothing at all on the long end.

Push the pencil towards the short end and the heavy end goes down. Move it back towards the middle and the ruler tips the other way.

**The heavy thing has not changed weight, the ruler has not changed length, and nothing has been lifted a single centimetre.**

<!-- STEP -->
## The trade is the same one

You are trading force against distance, exactly as the ramp did - the load went down a little so that the far end could go up a lot.

Which is the reason the ruler is on this slide and not the ramp: **there is no rope and no ground here.** No surface for the load to be pushed along, no length to measure, no angle to shallow out. The whole machine is a rigid bar and a point to turn it on.

<!-- STEP -->
## So the question the next lesson opens with

Every machine so far has taken something and given something back:

- a wheel that changed the direction of a pull,
- ropes that shared a load,
- a ramp that made a push smaller and a distance longer.

**A rigid bar trades something else.**

The point that bar turns on has a name, and you use one every time you prise something off the ground.
